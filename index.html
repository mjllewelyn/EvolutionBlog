Thoughts on Evolution
By Matt Llewelyn

Define Terms (simply)

  • Young Earth - God created everything in 6 literal 24 hour days and the Earth is approximately 6,000 - 10,000 years old
  • Old Earth - The days in Genesis are not literal days and God took longer to create everything. The age of the Earth is likely closer to the scientific estimate of millions to billions of years old
  • Macro-Evolution - The process where every species came from one common ancestor and evolved naturally over time
    • Christian Evolutionist perspective - God created the first life and then let things take their course naturally to eventually come to humans and gave humans souls
  • Micro-Evolution (scientific definition) - The process where only a species mutates into a different species, but no higher than species
    • As in a lion mutating into a tiger would not be scientifically micro-evolution, but macro-evolution
    • This is what Darwin observed with the finches on the Galapagos Islands
  • Micro-Evolution (Christian definition) - The process where one type/kind (scientific family) evolves over time into different species within that type/kind
    • As in God created one feline ancestor that evolved into all the cats (lions, tigers, lynxes, etc.) that we have today
    • Scientists would lump this type of evolution with their definition of macro-evolution

My Personal Opinion

First and foremost, I believe God created all things. Scripture is ambiguous as to the exact process God used to create everything, but holding to this fact is essential for Christianity. We may have differences in opinion, but if we can agree on the doctrine that God is the creator then these other facts are secondary.

I personally hold to an Old Earth view, where there is Christian Micro-Evolution, but not any form of Macro-Evolution.

Old Earther

There are issues with both Young and Old Earth, but I feel like the issues with Old Earth are more easily reconcilable. One issue is that the Earth just looks old in that there are fossils and rock formations that seem to be from a long time ago. Yes, I think there may be issues with the dating method used for the fossils, but the fact still remains that the Earth just looks old from observation. God is not a deceptive God, so why would he make something look really old if it was only a few thousand years old?

The Young Earth answer is that the Flood caused it because all the water caused massive erosion making things seem older than they appear. Also they say there was one continent (Pangea) before the Flood and that the Flood immediately caused all the continents to move into the positions they are today. These do answer the two fundamental questions of why the Earth looks old and why the same fossils seemingly appear on different continents. I'm not saying God couldn't have done this, but this still doesn't answer the question of why God would move continents thousands of miles but not preserve the Earth still looking young.

Another issue is with the speed of light if the Earth is only thousands of years old. If we see explosions of stars that are millions of light years away, how is that possible if that event happened millions of years ago and the light has just now reached Earth? It is possible for God to have changed the speed of light at some point, since anything is possible, but why would He? Again, the point is that God is not a deceptive God, so why would He lead us on with an apparent old Earth if it was indeed young?

The biggest issue with the Old Earth perspective is that there would likely be animal and plant death before the Fall. To me, this isn't a strong issue, but I know this is a stronger issue to some. I personally hold that the Death referred to in Genesis 3 is just relating to human death. God created humans in His image, and we are unique to all the other animals, so God has a relationship with humans unlike anything with the animals.

There is an indication that humans were vegetarian before the Flood (Genesis 9:2-3), which begs the question if animals were also vegetarian before the Fall. I think this is possible, but even if there were carnivores before the Fall, there still wouldn't be human death, just animal death. Or if an animal got caught in a river and drowned before the Fall, it would still be ok. God is in control of everything, so He could have also protected the animals from harm just like He protected Adam and Eve.

In Revelation, there is a reference to the Tree of Life and the Garden which seems like a "Return to the Garden of Eden" scene. Even if there was animal death in the Garden of Eden, I think that is still ok, since the Garden in Revelation is even more perfect than the Garden in Genesis. There will be no more sin or death in Revelation, which the Garden of Eden doesn't explicitly state that there is no animal death. Plus there was a way to sin and the evil serpent in Genesis which will not be in Revelation's garden. So I personally say that this reference is pointing to a time when things were good in Eden, but not yet perfect, and the new Garden will be the fulfillment of the original Garden which is perfect.

Like I said, there are issues with both sides, and more than what was covered here, but I tend to agree with the Old Earth side. It is possible to hold to either view and still have a good relationship with God. God didn't explicitly tell us one way or the other, and it's good for us to wrestle with these topics to better understand more about God during our wrestling.

Non-Macro-Evolutionist

As described above I would be an Old Earther, and that God took longer than 6 literal days to create the Earth, but I also wouldn't say He used Macro-Evolution to accomplish His plan. There are some issues with Macro-Evolution that are hard for me to mesh with the character of God.

The biggest issue is that Macro-Evolution diminishes the power of God. If God is all-powerful and can create literally anything from nothing, why did He just stop at the first lifeform? He then would just take a break, being absent and do nothing else until monkeys eventually became humans and then get involved again. This doesn't seem like the type of God that enjoys and loves His creation. Sure, God could have maybe helped out with a mutation here or there to speed it up, but that still seems more passive then how Scripture portrays our active and involved God.

In a way, the same thing could be said for any Old Earth theory, since God could have created everything all in a single instant. I do think God was actively involved in the entire creation process, and may have taken His time as a model for humans to be more intentional in our own creations. Sure, God could create everything perfectly in an instant, but we are not perfect, so when we work on something it's not perfect and takes time for us to understand and get right. Even the 7 days in Genesis is an example of working for 6 days and resting for 1. So God is intentional in all that He does even if we don't completely understand it.

As far as Christian Micro-Evolution, I would tend to support it, but don't have a strong opinion about it. I think there is a scriptural basis for God creating "kinds" of animals and plants (Genesis 1), and also specifying "kinds" of animals for the Flood (Genesis 6-9). I don't see an issue of this type of evolution diminishing the power or involvement of God. In this model, He created the first "kind" of each animal, and over time, there were a few species of each "kind" when God created humans. God is always in control and allowed each "kind" (and may have been directly involved) to mutate into different species and allowed artificial breeding for even more species.

I think we can all agree that the scientific Micro-Evolution does exist because it has been observed and God does allow these mutations to happen. Nothing happens without God knowing about it, so it is no accident that species evolve into other species and we can see this happen. I think there is also a reason that there are different types of cats, and not just one "kind" of feline. We may never know all the reasons God created this diverse world, but I think part of it is so that we can marvel at His creativity and power.

God is an amazing God! We will never truly understand God on this side of heaven, but we do know He is the creator of everything. We don't know all the specifics of how and when He created us, but He is in control and made us on purpose. With everything God has made, we can remember that we, as humans, are His most prized creation.
